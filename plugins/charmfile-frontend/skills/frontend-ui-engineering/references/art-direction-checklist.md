# Art Direction Checklist

Use this checklist for a new surface or major redesign. It adds visual authorship without replacing product truth, existing constraints, accessibility, or engineering rigor.

## 1. Identify the Surface Job

Choose the surface mode from the user's immediate goal:

- **Persuade:** understand an offer and act.
- **Operate:** complete a task accurately.
- **Read:** understand and navigate information.
- **Experience:** explore or encounter the work itself.

Let the mode set the hierarchy. A tool's marketing page still persuades; a branded dashboard still operates.

## 2. Refuse the Automatic Answer

Name the layout and aesthetic this category usually receives. When the brief does not require that answer, do not ship it by reflex.

Check for:

- a generic centered hero followed by repeated equal cards;
- fake product chrome where real content or interaction should be;
- gradients, glass, glows, pills, monospace, or editorial serifs used as category costumes;
- one visual treatment repeated at every section regardless of meaning;
- oversized type or media that delays the actual product;
- decorative labels, numbers, or icons that add no information.

These are not universal bans. A grounded brief may earn any device. Unexplained habit does not.

## 3. Ground the Visual World

Derive the visual language from at least two real inputs: the product mechanism, audience context, supplied assets, established brand, content structure, or physical/cultural world around the subject.

Choose:

- one color strategy: restrained, committed, full palette, or drenched;
- a type relationship that suits the content density and use scene;
- a component language with explicit border, radius, depth, and control behavior;
- one memorable compositional device that helps explain or operate the product.

Do not mix several fashionable worlds. Commit to one coherent grammar.

## 4. Write the Direction Contract

Record five short blocks before code:

1. **Thesis:** the owned idea and refused category default.
2. **World:** the recognizable palette, type, material, and component grammar.
3. **Story:** the user's understanding and action sequence.
4. **First viewport:** the exact composition, product proof, and primary action.
5. **Form:** the layout and interaction structure and why it fits.

Keep the contract under 120 words. Every block must describe something observable in the eventual render.

## 5. Build the First Viewport as Proof

The first viewport must show the product, subject, or workflow doing its job. It cannot be only atmosphere, a slogan, or an illustration detached from the task.

For persuasive or discovery surfaces:

- make the offer intelligible within seconds;
- show one clear primary action;
- put product-specific proof or useful interaction above generic claims;
- keep the primary action fully visible at 390 × 844;
- keep hero imagery subordinate to comprehension unless the imagery is the product.

For operational surfaces, show the task, state, and familiar affordance before brand theater.

## 6. Pace the Whole Surface

Vary density, scale, media, and quiet while retaining one visual grammar. Use cards only when containment or comparison helps the task. Let lists, timelines, tables, split layouts, annotated media, and open composition carry content when they fit better.

Use more space between ideas than inside a related group. Allow long text to wrap naturally. Keep display type below the point where it fragments the reading order.

## 7. Review Against the Contract

At desktop and mobile sizes, ask:

- Can someone describe the product rather than merely the mood after one viewport?
- Is the chosen world recognizable without relying on copy?
- Did the build preserve the product's real behavior and content?
- Does every major visual device help comprehension, action, or identity?
- Is any section present only because landing pages usually contain it?
- Did responsive adaptation preserve the concept instead of merely stacking it?

Fix contract failures before polishing shadows, animation, or decorative details.
